# For Rich: the metaveillography of the human eye, and where it touches healthcare

Prepared 2026-10-13 for the Eye as a Camera in XR paper (ICCE 2027). These are the
papers in Steve Mann's "seeing sight itself" programme that image the human eye's
own sensing, in the order the ideas appeared, with the health hook of each. The
paper's Background section now carries a paragraph on this lineage and cites all of
them; the Section V opening ties them to the screening argument.

## The vocabulary, in one paragraph

Veillance is sensing (sur-veillance from above, sous-veillance from below).
Metaveillance is the sensing of sensors and of their capacity to sense. A
sightfield is a camera's or an eye's capacity to see, drawn in space: Mann's 1975
demonstration waved a light bulb that brightened when a camera saw it and
photographed it over a long exposure. Veillance flux is that capacity made
quantitative (vixels, veillons). Veillametrics is its measurement. A veillogram is
the flux time-integrated onto an object, as if the object were photographic film. A
metaveillograph is the photograph; metaveillogrammetry is the measurement from it.
Applied to the eye with the EEG as the sensor, the result is a photograph of the
eye's own receptive field that the subject never had to describe: evidence rather
than testimony. That last phrase is the one that matters for community health work.

## The papers

1. **S. Mann, "The Sightfield: Visualizing Computer Vision, and Seeing Its Capacity to
   'See'," IEEE CVPR Workshops 2014, pp. 618-623, doi 10.1109/CVPRW.2014.127.**
   Defines the sightfield as a time-reversed lightfield and shows it with
   long-exposure photography and a wand whose lights brighten when watched. The
   method is general: anything that senses can be photographed sensing.

2. **R. Janzen and S. Mann, "Veillance Flux, Vixels, Veillons: An Information-Bearing
   Extramissive Formulation of Sensing, to Measure Surveillance and Sousveillance,"
   IEEE CCECE 2014.** The quantitative formulation: sensing treated as a flux that
   can be measured, summed and compared across sensors, electronic or biological.

3. **R. Janzen and S. Mann, "Sensory Flux from the Eye: Biological Sensing-of-Sensing
   (Veillametrics) for 3D Augmented-Reality Environments," IEEE GEM 2015, pp. 205-213,
   doi 10.1109/GEM.2015.7377217.** The first measurement of how far the human eye's
   ability to see extends through space, as a biological veillance flux, with far
   more detail than gaze tracking's single point. Proposed uses: an AR visualisation
   of human sight, a score of how much a target is seen, a precision metric for
   control panels. Health hook: it is a visual-field measurement made from outside
   the subject.

4. **S. Mann, "Surveillance (Oversight), Sousveillance (Undersight), and Metaveillance
   (Seeing Sight Itself)," IEEE CVPR Workshops 2016, pp. 1408-1417,
   doi 10.1109/CVPRW.2016.177.** Names metaveillance, introduces the veillance
   wavefunction, and extends the idea to wearable and mobile sensors.

5. **R. Janzen, S. Yang and S. Mann, "'Painting with the Eyes': Sensory Perception Flux
   Time-Integrated on the Physical World," IEEE GEM 2018, pp. 239-245,
   doi 10.1109/GEM.2018.8516540.** The veillogram: the gaze's flux accumulated on
   objects over time, a sensory map rather than a gaze point. The paper itself lists
   medical applications alongside cockpit, control-room and interface design.

6. **S. Mann et al., "The Human Eye as a Camera," IEEE HealthCom 2019.** The paper our
   work descends from, and the one published at a health venue. Two things in it:
   (a) metaveillography of the eye with the brain as the reporter, a brain-modulated
   RGB LED carried through the visual field by a 3D plotter, its colour and
   brightness set by the SSVEP read from a Muse headband with an added occipital
   electrode, photographed over a long exposure into the eye's receptive field
   (its Fig. 4); and (b) a Health Applications section that says the veillance
   field is closer to the spotlight of attention than to the optics: it narrows
   when the observer fixates and ignores the flicker, widens when they track it.
   The paper calls this "the vision test of the future" because it includes brain
   function, and proposes it as a predictive biomarker of attention problems in
   ADHD and ageing, hence a target for intervention. It also describes a wearable
   (SSVEPVMP) that flickers the world at 15 Hz and predicts which everyday events
   the wearer attended to, 89% hit rate in a first test. That is the JITAI seed.

7. **S. Mann et al., "Eye Itself as a Camera: Sensors, Integrity, and Trust," ACM WearSys
   2019, doi 10.1145/3325424.3330210.** The keynote abstract that first stated the
   evidence-not-testimony framing (the "ayinograph").

8. **D. E. Garcia, K. W. Zheng, Y. Liu, Y. S. Tao and S. Mann, "Painting with the Eye:
   Understanding the Visual Field of the Human Eye with SSVEP," IEEE SMC 2020,
   pp. 3689-3694, doi 10.1109/SMC42975.2020.9283476.** How the SSVEP depends on a
   flashing stimulus's size, distance, shape and position; wearable EEG with an
   external occipital electrode, lock-in and FFT; central vision responds more than
   peripheral; the result is a "vidmap" of the participant's visual ability. Health
   hook: this is perimetry without a button.

9. **D. E. Garcia, Y. Liu, K. W. Zheng, Y. S. Tao, P. V. Do, C. Pierce and S. Mann,
   "Interpreting the Visual Acuity of the Human Eye with Wearable EEG Device and
   SSVEP," in IoT Technologies for HealthCare (LNICST), Springer, 2021, pp. 77-92,
   doi 10.1007/978-3-030-69963-5_6.** Acuity from the SSVEP with the same set-up,
   published in a healthcare IoT volume. Our sealed tumbling-E and O/U read-out is
   the direct descendant of this chapter.

10. **S. Mann, S. Khaki, J. Bhimani, G. Verges and F. Sadrzadeh-Afsharazar, "Sensing and
    Sensing-of-Sensing with Drones," ICECCME 2021, pp. 1-6,
    doi 10.1109/ICECCME52200.2021.9590939.** The terms metaveillography and
    metaveillogrammetry as a pair (reciprocals of veillography and
    veillogrammetry), with drone swarms carrying the light.

Also relevant: R. Janzen and S. Mann, "Quantifying the Politics and Physics of
Ubiquitous Sensing, Using Veillance Flux," IEEE Pervasive Computing 19(2), 2020;
and Janzen's University of Toronto thesis, "Extended Intelligence and Mediated
Reality: Veillametrics in the Space, Time, Frequency, and Amplitude Domains."

## How the ICCE paper uses this

- The Background paragraph "Metaveillography of the eye" places our pixel-by-pixel
  images, colour planes and acuity read-out inside this lineage and states the two
  health claims it inherits: the field is an attention measure, and the read-out is
  evidence rather than testimony.
- Section V opens with the three readings the lineage defines (visual field, acuity,
  attention state) and the hardware cost.
- What our paper adds to the lineage: consumer hardware end to end (Quest browser,
  no plotter, no lab amplifier), frame-exact delivered-frequency bookkeeping, colour
  by calibrated planes, a sealed acuity decision with a chance level, and eye strain
  from the same recording with a prompt as the intervention.

## Where Rich's work meets it

- Objective, worker-operated screening on phone-class hardware (pulmonary, child
  health, printed diagnostics, thermal imaging): the metaveillograph is the vision
  screen in that style, and the only one where the subject reports nothing.
- Wearables as a window on state (iCalm, the CBT apps, JITAI): the attention reading
  of the veillance field and our blink-release eye-strain reading are the state
  signals; the "rest your eyes" prompt and, later, an attention prompt are the
  interventions.
- Fairness and appropriate use of AI in global health: a read-out that needs no
  literacy, no language and no self-report removes one axis of bias at the source.
